Consider the problem of estimating a parameter $\theta$ from a set of observations $x_1, \ldots, x_n$ drawn independently from a distribution $p(x \mid \theta)$. The likelihood function is

$$
L(\theta) = \prod_{i=1}^{n} p(x_i \mid \theta)
$$

and we typically work with the log-likelihood $\ell(\theta) = \sum_{i=1}^{n} \log p(x_i \mid \theta)$ for computational convenience. The maximum likelihood estimator $\hat{\theta}_{\text{MLE}}$ is the value of $\theta$ that maximises this quantity.

A natural question is how precisely we can estimate $\theta$ given $n$ observations. The Cramér-Rao lower bound gives a fundamental limit. For any unbiased estimator $\hat{\theta}$, the variance satisfies

$$
\text{Var}(\hat{\theta}) \geq \frac{1}{I(\theta)}
$$

where $I(\theta)$ is the Fisher information, defined as

$$
I(\theta) = -\mathbb{E}\left[\frac{\partial^2}{\partial \theta^2} \log p(X \mid \theta)\right].
$$

This tells us that the curvature of the log-likelihood around the true parameter value controls the best achievable precision. Flat log-likelihoods mean imprecise estimation; sharply peaked ones mean the data are informative about $\theta$.

In the Bayesian setting we instead place a prior $\pi(\theta)$ and compute the posterior via Bayes' theorem:

$$
p(\theta \mid x_{1:n}) = \frac{\prod_{i=1}^{n} p(x_i \mid \theta) \, \pi(\theta)}{\int \prod_{i=1}^{n} p(x_i \mid \theta') \, \pi(\theta') \, d\theta'}.
$$

The denominator — the marginal likelihood or evidence — is typically intractable, which motivates the use of MCMC methods for approximate posterior computation. In the large-sample limit, the posterior concentrates around $\hat{\theta}_{\text{MLE}}$ with variance approximately $I(\theta)^{-1}/n$, recovering the frequentist result as a special case.

Finally, for a multivariate parameter $\boldsymbol{\theta} \in \mathbb{R}^d$, the Fisher information generalises to a $d \times d$ matrix with entries

$$
[I(\boldsymbol{\theta})]_{jk} = -\mathbb{E}\left[\frac{\partial^2}{\partial \theta_j \, \partial \theta_k} \log p(X \mid \boldsymbol{\theta})\right]
$$

and the Cramér-Rao bound becomes $\text{Cov}(\hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}) \succeq I(\boldsymbol{\theta})^{-1}$, where the inequality is in the positive semidefinite sense.
