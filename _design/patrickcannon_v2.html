I'm a machine learning researcher working on multimodal speech generation at Amazon, with a focus on building systems that are robust, well-calibrated, and honest about what they don't know.

My research sits at the intersection of probabilistic machine learning and deep learning — specifically, how to make neural systems trustworthy enough to deploy in high-stakes settings. I've worked on simulation-based inference, normalising flows, and uncertainty quantification across scientific and applied domains, including at Improbable and through my own NeRF startup.

I believe alignment is the defining technical challenge of our moment. A model that is capable but untrustworthy is a model that shouldn't exist. My PhD in statistics at Bristol — focused on MCMC inference for population genetic models — gave me a rigorous grounding in the kinds of probabilistic reasoning that I think will be central to solving it.
